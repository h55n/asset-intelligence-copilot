How it works

Ask in plain words.
Get the records back.

01

Ask anything

Type a question the way you would ask a colleague: “Is WELL-0001 producing oil? Why?” or “Any overdue spill reports?” You can also tell it field updates to remember, like a corrected temperature or a planned shut-in.

02

It checks the records

The copilot looks up the actual inspections, temperatures, spill reports, repair orders and compliance manuals. Deterministic rules parsed from the manuals - the 15°C and 20°C thresholds, the 24-hour containment window - decide the verdicts, not the model's mood.

03

You get the answer, cited

The answer comes back in plain language with the exact record IDs underneath - INS-0001-0203, CTX-0001-003, T200 manual §9.3. Tap any citation to pull the full record and check it yourself.

When people disagree with the data

It keeps both on record -
and says which one the data supports.

Field knowledge lives in people before it reaches the records. The copilot never silently picks a side: every claim is stored with its author's name, checked against the latest data, and surfaced when they conflict.

Michael Turner, Monday
“WELL-0175 has been shut in for planned work since 14 Oct.”
Tyler Hughes, Tuesday
“WELL-0175 is running fine.”
The copilot, to Tyler
Recorded - but the 15 Oct inspection by Jessica Reed shows the wellhead only 0.4°C above air, so WELL-0175 is not producing. That matches Michael's note, not yours. Both statements stay on record, yours marked unconfirmed - can you double-check on site?
